# Art Techniques & Mediums Reference

How to recognize and replicate each technique digitally.

---

## Traditional Mediums

### Oil Paint
**Look:** Rich depth from glazing (transparent layers), impasto (thick texture), slow drying allows blending, luminous color.
**Digital replication:** Layer multiply blending, soft brush with low opacity for glazes, thick smear brush for impasto, warm-cool temperature shifts within single color area.

### Watercolor
**Look:** Transparent washes, wet-into-wet blooms (cauliflower edges), granulation (pigment separating from water), white is the paper showing through, unpredictable soft edges.
**Digital replication:** Transparent layers, soft irregular edges, paper texture underneath, never use white paint — mask out highlights, granulation texture overlay.

### Gouache
**Look:** Opaque watercolor, flat even coverage, matte finish, colors look lighter when dry, can paint light over dark (unlike watercolor).
**Digital replication:** Flat opaque color fills, slight texture, no glazing effect, matte finish (no glossy highlights), illustrative quality.

### Acrylic
**Look:** Can mimic oil or watercolor depending on dilution, fast drying, plastic quality when thick, versatile.
**Digital replication:** Depends on how used — see oil or watercolor above.

### Pastel
**Look:** Soft powdery texture, blended with finger creating soft gradients, rich pigment, paper texture visible, velvety surface.
**Digital replication:** Soft chalk brush, paper grain texture overlay, soft edges everywhere, slightly desaturated "dusty" quality.

### Charcoal / Graphite
**Look:** Velvety blacks, smudgeable, erasure creates highlights, directional hatching, gestural marks, paper texture.
**Digital replication:** Dark grey (not black) lines, smear brush for blending, erase for highlights, hatching marks in consistent direction, paper texture overlay.

### Fresco
**Look:** Matte flat color, painted into wet plaster so color sinks in, slightly faded appearance, plaster cracks over time, wall-scale.
**Digital replication:** Matte palette with slightly faded/chalky quality, plaster texture overlay, slightly desaturated and warm.

### Encaustic
**Look:** Beeswax-based, luminous translucency, thick built-up layers, ancient Egyptian mummy portraits, slightly rough encrusted surface.
**Digital replication:** Warm translucent layers, waxy sheen, textured surface, warm amber-toned.

## Drawing Techniques

### Hatching & Cross-hatching
**Look:** Parallel lines building tonal value, denser lines = darker value, cross-hatching for darker areas, Dürer, Rembrandt drawings.
**Digital replication:** Lines in consistent direction for light areas, perpendicular crossing lines for shadow, line weight varies with pressure.

### Contour Drawing
**Look:** Single continuous line following form edges, no shading, pure line quality, variable weight.
**Digital replication:** Variable-width stroke, no fill or shadow, clean precise line.

### Gesture Drawing
**Look:** Rapid marks capturing movement and energy, not detail, loose flowing lines, Matisse line drawings.
**Digital replication:** Loose confident single strokes, no overworking, motion implied in line direction.

### Sfumato
**Look:** Smoke-like tonal transitions, no perceptible edge between areas, Leonardo da Vinci's signature.
**Digital replication:** Gaussian blur on all transitions (8–15px), low opacity brush builds up slowly, no hard edges anywhere in composition.

### Chiaroscuro
**Look:** Strong light/dark contrast to create volume, dramatic single light source, Baroque technique.
**Digital replication:** High contrast (near-black shadows, bright highlight), single directional light, soft transition in midtone zone only.

### Tenebrism
**Look:** Extreme chiaroscuro — figures emerge from near-total darkness, Caravaggio's signature, even more dramatic than chiaroscuro.
**Digital replication:** Background nearly black (#0D0A06), figures lit by one strong off-frame source, very small zone of midtone.

## Printmaking Techniques

### Woodblock / Woodcut
**Look:** Bold black lines, no subtle gradients, carved away areas are white, rough fibrous edge quality, Japanese ukiyo-e prints.
**Digital replication:** High contrast, no grey midtones, rough irregular edges on lines, flat color fills, bold simplified shapes.

### Etching
**Look:** Fine delicate lines bitten into metal plate, dense cross-hatching for tone, plate tone (slight grey wash), Rembrandt etchings.
**Digital replication:** Fine hairlines, dense hatching for shadow, slight grey plate tone overlay, intimate small-scale quality.

### Lithography
**Look:** Greasy crayon on stone, soft tonal range, slightly grainy texture, can achieve photographic quality.
**Digital replication:** Soft grainy texture, full tonal range, slightly warm grey quality.

### Screen Print / Silkscreen
**Look:** Flat color layers, misregistration between layers as aesthetic, halftone dots for gradients, each color separate pass, Warhol.
**Digital replication:** Flat color layers on separate layers, slight offset between colors, halftone dot pattern for gradients.

### Linocut
**Look:** Bold simplified shapes, strong black and white, V-shaped gouged marks visible in black areas, coarser than woodcut.
**Digital replication:** Very bold black and white, visible gouge mark texture in filled areas, simplified strong shapes.

## Composition Techniques from Art History

### Golden Ratio / Spiral
Fibonacci spiral guides eye through composition. Focal point at approximately 0.618 from edge.
`Golden ratio: 1 : 1.618`
Place key elements at golden spiral intersections. Nautilus shell shape as guide.

### Rule of Thirds
Canvas divided in 3×3 grid. Focal points at four intersection nodes.
Strong horizontal elements align with thirds lines. Horizon on upper or lower third (not center).

### Triangle Composition
Renaissance favorite. Three figures or focal points form implied triangle. Creates stability and balance.
Apex at top (stable) or inverted (dynamic tension).

### Diagonal Composition
Baroque favorite. Major elements align along diagonal from corner to corner.
Creates drama and movement vs. horizontal stability.

### Radial Composition
Elements arranged around central focal point. Eye drawn to center and held there.
Used in: rose windows, Baroque ceiling paintings, mandalas.

### Frieze Composition
Figures arranged in horizontal band parallel to picture plane. Classical Greek temples, Egyptian reliefs.
Flat, processional, no deep illusionistic space.

### S-Curve / Serpentine
Figure or compositional flow traces an S through picture. Creates graceful movement.
Mannerist figures, Baroque drapery, landscape paths.

## Color Techniques from Art History

### Simultaneous Contrast (Chevreul)
Adjacent complementary colors intensify each other. Red next to green looks more red and more green.
Use: Place small accent color next to its complement for maximum vibrancy.

### Optical Mixing (Seurat/Pointillism)
Tiny dots of pure color placed adjacent — eye mixes them at distance.
Red + blue dots read as purple from far away.
Use: Place alternating pure color pixels for optical blend effect.

### Color Temperature Modeling (Cézanne)
Instead of light/shadow, use warm/cool to model form.
Lit areas: warm (yellow, orange). Shadow areas: cool (blue, violet).
Transition between warm and cool reads as edge of form.

### Aerial/Atmospheric Perspective
Objects in distance appear: lighter in value, bluer/cooler in hue, lower in contrast, less detailed.
Leonardo codified this. Use: desaturate + shift to blue + reduce contrast for distant elements.

### Color as Emotion (Expressionism)
Color chosen for psychological effect, not naturalistic accuracy.
Red = danger/passion, Blue = melancholy, Yellow = anxiety (Van Gogh), Green = sickness.
